For many small and medium businesses, a reliable network is the backbone of daily operations. While enterprise IT can become complex, understanding a few basic networking concepts is incredibly helpful when discussing your infrastructure setup.

### Core Devices

1. **Switches:** A switch connects multiple devices (like computers, printers, and servers) together on the same Local Area Network (LAN). It acts as a smart controller, directing data only to the specific device that requested it, rather than broadcasting it to everyone.
    
2. **Routers:** While a switch connects devices _within_ a network, a router connects multiple networks _together_. Your business router connects your internal office network (LAN) to the wider Internet (WAN). It acts as the gateway and director for data leaving your building.
    

### IP Addressing and Subnets

Every device on a network needs a unique identifier to communicate, known as an IP Address (e.g., 192.168.1.50).

- **DHCP:** Instead of manually assigning these addresses, a DHCP server (often built into the router) automatically hands out IP addresses to devices as they connect.
    
- **Subnetting:** Think of a subnet as dividing a large office building into smaller departments. Subnetting breaks a large network down into smaller, more manageable, and more secure segments (for example, keeping the guest Wi-Fi on a separate subnet from the accounting servers).
